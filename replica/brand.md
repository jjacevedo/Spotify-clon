Final, 2026-10-04. /replica-brand phase. The founder chose the name **Tunehold** and the palette **A · Dusk**. This file covers the name and its checks, the palette, the logo brief, the voice, and the rebrand sweep. ## Name

### Tunehold

- **Meaning.** "Tune" plus "hold". A hold is where cargo is kept, and holdings are what someone owns; the word is patterned on "household". Tunehold reads as *the place your own music is kept*, which is angle A in `replica/fixes.md` section 6: own it, play it everywhere.
- **Why this name.** Of the eight names screened on 2026-10-04, it is the only finalist with nothing identical in any register, store or web check that ran, and its exact .com, .net and GitHub name were all free at check time. It is easy to spell and say in English and Spanish, and it stays in the codename's Tune- family.
- **Pronunciation.** TOON-hohld (/ˈtuːn.hoʊld/), stress on the first syllable. Two syllables.
- **Spelling.** One word with one capital: **Tunehold**. Not "TuneHold" or "Tune Hold". Splitting the word makes "hold" stand alone, which invites the hold-music reading (below).
- **Verdict from the screening: caution.** Nothing identical turned up in any check that ran. Still open: TUNEHOOD (pending US application, class 41, one letter away), the weak and crowded TUNE- stem, which leaves HOLD to carry the mark, and the "hold music" reading, which is a positioning risk, not a legal one.

### Caution: TUNEHOOD

TUNEHOOD, US serial number 99899715, is a live **pending** application filed 2026-06-23 by Tunehood LLC (St. Petersburg, FL; it has an attorney). It covers class 41, a music and entertainment information and music-discovery website, and was still awaiting examination at check time. It is in use at tampa.tunehood.com with a TM symbol. It is one letter away from TUNEHOLD, sounds close, and is in music. It was not found in Canada (CIPO) or the App Store.

What softens it: it is pending, in class 41 only, and differs in meaning (hood vs hold), and the USPTO already lets 212 live TUNE- marks coexist in classes 9 and 42. What it means in practice: a trademark lawyer must weigh it for class 41, and possibly for 9 and 42, before anything is filed or spent. Search TUNEHOOD as well as TUNEHOLD in the EU register.

### Risk: the "hold music" reading

Next to music, "hold" strongly evokes telephone hold music, and "on hold" suggests waiting. In the judge pass on 2026-10-04, a web search for the name surfaced Wikipedia's "Music on hold" article. No "TuneHold" hold-music service exists, and the meaning is not a legal issue, but it is a positioning risk. How the brand steers away from it:

- The voice never uses hold puns: no "on hold", "hold on", "please hold", "hold tight" or "hold music", in product copy, ads or social posts.
- Copy talks about keeping and playing your files ("your library", "kept as you uploaded it"), not about holding.
- The logo shows something kept, never waiting: no telephone handset, hourglass, pause sign or clock (Logo brief).
- Before money is spent, run the 5-listener test: say the name once to five people who have not seen it, and ask what kind of product they picture. If most say hold music or a phone system, revisit the name with the runner-up below.

**Watch-out: Deezer purple.** Since its November 2023 rebrand, Deezer has made a saturated purple its signature colour, with a heart-shaped logo, and says it wants purple to stand for Deezer the way green stands for the original ([Deezer newsroom](https://newsroom-deezer.com/2023/11/deezer-new-brand-identity-and-logo/), [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/deezer-rebrand)). Violet is therefore not empty ground in music. Dusk stays apart because it is a pale, low-chroma blue-violet (HSL 250° against Deezer's 272°; Delta-E 63 for the dark accent), used flat on near-black and paired with apricot. Keep it that way: no saturated electric violet, no purple-to-pink or multicolour sets, and no heart in the logo.

### Usage rules

- **Dark first.** The app ships the `color` block. `color-light` is for the landing page (and a light mode later, if it is ever added).
- **No gradients** on the brand colours. They would echo Jellyfin's purple-to-blue gradient and the generic purple-gradient look. Flat fills only.
- **Green stays small.** `success` is for ticks and short status text, never a button, a large fill or a brand moment, so nothing reads as green. No green-on-black anywhere.
- **Every fill has its text colour.** Primary buttons are `accent` with `on-accent` text. Danger buttons are `danger` with `on-accent` text. The "new" badge is `accent-2` with `on-accent` text.
- **`accent-2` is a highlight, not a theme.** Small marks only (now-playing marker, badges, selection marker), never a page band or a large fill.
- **Borders are not controls.** Any edge that tells someone where to click or type uses `border-input` or `accent`, not `border`.
- **No hardcoded colours in code.** Use the token names; re-run contrast.py on both blocks after any change.
- Colour alone is not trademark clearance. Put the logo drafts next to the neighbours' marks (Logo brief) before committing.

## Logo brief

A brief for whoever makes the logo (the founder, a designer or an image model). Not a logo.

- **The idea in one line.** *A hold is where you keep what's yours:* an open crate holding one record sleeve, standing for your own music, kept and ready to play on the web and on your phone.
- **Why it fits.** It ties the name (a hold, holdings) to angle A (people who own their music, CD rips and purchases, not a catalogue). A crate of records is the collector's own shelf, not a store.
- **Mark type: symbol plus wordmark.** The symbol alone is the app icon and the favicon. The wordmark is "Tunehold", one word, one capital; start from Atkinson Hyperlegible Next Bold and adjust the spacing and letterforms so it is the brand's own. Do not box, colour or underline "hold" or "tune" separately.
- **The symbol.** A crate seen from the front: straight sides, a flat bottom, slightly rounded corners, open at the top, drawn with a thick stroke. One square sleeve stands in it with its top edge above the rim. Square and straight-edged throughout, not round. Crate in `accent` (`#AC9CFA` on dark), sleeve in `accent-2` (`#FFB48C`), or one colour.
- **Sizes.**
  - It must read at **16px** as a favicon: at that size it reduces to two shapes (the open crate and the square), with no thin lines or inner detail. Test at 16, 32 and 48px.
  - It must hold up as a **1024px** app icon: the symbol centred on `#100E17` (or on `#5E3DCB` for the light variant), inside the platform safe areas.
- **Deliverables.**
  - SVG: symbol, wordmark, horizontal and stacked lockups, each in full colour on dark, full colour on light, and one colour (white, black).
  - iOS app icon: 1024x1024 PNG, **no transparency**, square corners (iOS applies the mask).
  - Android: Google Play icon 512x512 PNG and an adaptive icon (separate foreground and background layers).
  - Favicon set: favicon.ico (16, 32, 48), favicon.svg, apple-touch-icon 180x180, 192x192 and 512x512 PNG for the web manifest, plus a maskable 512x512.
  - Social image: 1200x630 PNG for Open Graph, with the lockup and one plain line on `#100E17`. No reviewer quotes, no screenshots of other apps.
- **Must not resemble the original's mark.**
  - No circle with three curved bars, and no arcs, sound waves or bars of any kind.
  - No green, and no green-on-black pairing.
  - No round disc or circular badge as the main shape.
  - Put the original's logo next to every draft and check shape, colour pair and letterform tricks.
- **Must not resemble other music marks** (descriptions from memory; compare with the current logos, not this text):
  - TuneIn: no wordmark with part of the name set inside a box or tag.
  - Tidal: no clusters of diamonds or rhombuses, no stark black-and-white geometric mark.
  - Plex and Plexamp: no chevron or play-arrow, and no amber or yellow. The apricot `accent-2` (Delta-E 43 from Plex amber) is fine as the small second colour, never as the main colour of the mark.
  - Deezer: no heart shape, no saturated purple.
  - Jellyfin: no gradient triangle.
- **Must not say "waiting".** No telephone handset, hourglass, pause sign, clock or headset (the hold-music reading).
- **Check before choosing.** View the drafts side by side with the marks above at 16px and at 1024px, and in greyscale. If a first-time viewer could mistake it for any of them, redraw. If an image model makes the drafts, check its terms for commercial use and ownership of the output.

## Voice

### Three words

| word | what it means | what it does NOT mean |
|---|---|---|
| **Plain** | Short words, the fact first, one idea per sentence. "3 files didn't fit." | **Not bare.** Still a full, friendly sentence, never an error code or a terse label on its own ("Error 413", "Failed"). |
| **Calm** | No hype, no exclamation marks, no urgency, no nagging. Upsells appear only at a real limit, and only once. | **Not passive.** It still says clearly what to do next, and it gives bad news straight (what was deleted, what will be, when). |
| **Exact** | Numbers, dates and what happens to the files: "{used} of {quota} used", "until {date}", "stored as you uploaded it". | **Not technical.** No jargon (transcode, bitrate, entitlement, quota, sync conflict) unless the person went looking for it in settings or help. |

The former angle B ("your music, your rules") lives here as tone only. Phrases that fit: *your files*, *in your order*, *only what you added*, *stored as you uploaded it*, *take it with you*, *offline on your phone*. Use them in product copy and onboarding. Never use them as a headline, a proof point or in ads, and never set them against any other service.

### Five do / don't pairs

| do | don't |
|---|---|
| **Say what happens to the files, with its limits.** "Tunehold stores each file as you uploaded it." Link "What we keep, and for how long" to the retention policy. | **Promise forever or without limits.** "Safe forever", "never lost", "unlimited storage", "never held hostage". No published policy backs these. |
| **Talk about the person's own collection.** "Your files", "only what you added", "in your order", "your library". | **Talk like a catalogue.** "Millions of songs", "discover new music", "streaming service", "ad-free music". The only ad line allowed is "no ads in your library, on any plan". |
| **Use numbers, dates and real plan terms.** "{used} of {quota} used." "You keep {plan} until {date}." | **Use urgency or scarcity.** "Hurry", "today only", "You're running out!", "Upgrade now", countdown timers. |
| **In errors: say what happened, say the file is fine (when it is), give one next step.** "{track} didn't load. Your file is still in your library." | **Blame or hide.** "Invalid file", "Oops! Something went wrong", jokes or emoji in an error. |
| **Ask once, when it matters (a full library, a cancel), and make "no" a real button.** | **Nag or guilt.** Repeated upgrade prompts, "We'll miss you", confirmshaming ("No thanks, I don't care about my music"), extra screens before a cancel goes through. |

A sixth rule that comes with the name: **no hold puns.** Never "on hold", "hold on", "please hold", "hold tight" or "hold music". Say *keep*, *your library* or *stored*.

### Bounded wording (every string, from fixes.md section 7)

- No "never", "unlimited", "forever" or "truly random" unless a published policy backs it. None does yet.
- Keep-claims stay bounded: the stored copy is kept bit for bit until the person deletes it, access to it is disabled after a valid copyright notice, or the account and retention policy applies (over-quota and inactive-account deletion after notice). In the UI, say "stored as you uploaded it" and link to the policy for the rest.
- Never name the original or any other service in product copy, ads or the listing. No "replacement for", no comparisons.
- No reviewer quotes, names, star counts or screenshots anywhere.
- No claims about paying artists, "fair to artists" or "ethical streaming".
- Never invite music people don't hold the rights to (no "grab any song", "from YouTube", "from anywhere").
- The original's feature and tier names stay out: Liked Songs, Your Library (as a label), Smart Shuffle, Wrapped, Daily Mix, Discover Weekly, Jam, Premium (Individual, Duo, Family). They are listed in `brand.json` `labels_to_avoid`.
- Upgrade buttons on mobile follow the store billing route (fixes.md section 7). If the apps are companions with no in-app sale, mobile strings drop every plan or price mention.
- Strings are written in US English. The launch countries that /replica-launch names decide which locales follow.
