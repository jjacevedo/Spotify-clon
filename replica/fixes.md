# tunebox: what users of the original hate, miss and leave unsolved, and what tunebox does about it

Final, 2026-10-04. /replica-entrepreneur research document. Three analyst lenses (evidence, fit, market) coded the same data, then three adversarial verifiers (quotes, counts, fit) checked the draft. Every verifier finding is applied, and so are the findings of two independent audits of the final document (7 each); section 8 lists all three rounds. tunebox has no licensed catalogue. It only plays files the user uploaded (web, iOS, Android).

Reviewers' words below are research, never testimonials: none of them may appear on a landing page, store listing or ad. Every review quote (curly quotes followed by a link) is an exact substring of the `text` cell of `replica/reviews.csv` for the linked row. Text copied from official pages is shown between « and » with its URL and date. This document names the original because it is research; tunebox's name, ads and store listing must not.

**Not reached:** Reddit, Trustpilot and the original's community board blocked access from this session. Nothing from them is in the data or implied anywhere here. For the market facts, several vendor pages (Google, Apple and Plex support pages, navidrome.org, ibroadcast.com, vox.rocks) were also unreachable, so those facts come from official store listings, official docs repositories, HN users quoting the official text, or HN submission titles (each marked in `analysis/facts/upload-market.verified.json`).

**Apple RSS capture:** the RSS edge cache served only some pages, so 34 of the 60 possible pages (6 storefronts × pages 1–10) were captured: us 8, nz 8, gb 6, ca 6, ie 4, au 2. The App Store slice is therefore the most recent reviews from those pages, not a random sample. It bunches in September–October 2026 and over-weights one-day spikes (22 of the 25 login complaints were posted on 2026-09-29).

**Method:**
- Three analysts each read the reviews by hand and used keyword nets only to find candidates. The **evidence** lens read all 594 "core" reviews (store reviews rated 1–3★: app-store 409, google-play 46; plus all 139 HN comments). It also scanned all 633 reviews rated 4–5★ (app-store 619, google-play 14) for *love it, but…* complaints in 25 themes. It checked the precision and recall of every keyword set against its hand labels. The **fit** and **market** lenses hand-coded all 1227 rows with their own codebooks.
- **Numbers below are the evidence lens's**, except where the independent recount (next bullet) found rows outside a theme's own definition. Those figures are the recount's and are marked *recount*. Counts are written as *n = core + 4–5★*. "4–5★ not scanned" means that theme was only counted in core. Themes are multi-label, so counts don't add up to the sample. Counts are reviews, not people.
- **Review pass (three adversarial checks).** *Quotes:* re-read the full row behind all 88 review quotes in the draft; all were exact, 3 failed on context and were replaced, 3 got an attribution note. *Counts:* one verifier read all 1227 rows in full with its own codebook (`analysis/verify/labels.txt`), re-read every row where its labels and the evidence lens's lists disagreed, and recorded each accept or reject with a reason (`analysis/verify/vcorrect.py`, output `analysis/verify-counts.json`). *Fit:* hand-read the own-files store rows, the injection, offline and shuffle themes and every owner-signal keyword hit, and fetched 4 more official support pages on 2026-10-04 (Autoplay, Understanding your data, Data rights and privacy choices, Recent activity; `analysis/verify-fit-raw/`). I spot-checked the recount's disputed rows for injection, library tools, clutter, icon, lossless, car, upload and discovery against `reviews.csv` and found no reason to depart from its figures.
- **Synthesizer re-run** (`analysis/synth/`): `compare.py` joins the three lenses theme by theme on review URL. The fit lens uses 1-based row ids and the other two use 0-based ids, so URL is the only safe join key. The 160 unique rows that fit or market placed in a theme where the evidence lens did not were re-read across the 8 most disputed themes (`adjudicate.py`); 4 were added (2 to playlist injection, 2 to free-tier control). `recount.py` re-derives the evidence-lens counts, source splits and date splits (`recount.json`). `extra_themes.py` counts two small themes used only in section 4. `quotes.py` verifies every quote.
- **Thin rule:** fewer than 3 reviews, or only one source, is marked **thin**. A theme whose store rows, on reading, do not actually express the theme is treated as single-source; this applies to upload demand (section 3, item 1). Thin themes are never presented as trends. The rule is applied the same way to tunebox's own bet (Angle A) and to ideas set aside (the Subsonic-compatible API).
- **Can tunebox address it?** "Yes" means tunebox owns that part of its own product (player, library, UX, pricing). "No, position only" means the complaint comes from the licensed catalogue, the ads business, artist payouts or politics. tunebox can only position against those, carefully.
- **Where the audit trail lives.** File paths in this document are relative to `replica/`. The analysis was done in the session scratchpad; on 2026-10-04 the files this document cites, and the analyst count files the synthesizer scripts read, were copied into `replica/analysis/` with the same layout (the two verified fact files into `replica/analysis/facts/`). The proposed features.csv rows and notes are in `analysis/features-rows.final.json` until a later phase applies them. Of the 4 support pages the fit check fetched, only the fetch log and the extracted text are copied (`analysis/verify-fit-raw/fetch_log.json`, `texts.json`), not the raw HTML. The scripts read `reviews.csv` from the absolute path `/home/user/Spotify-clon/replica/reviews.csv`. Run `vcorrect.py` from inside `analysis/verify/`. Run `quotes.py` from inside `analysis/synth/` with `--draft ../../fixes.md`: its usage line still names the scratchpad draft (`../fixes.draft.md`), which was not copied. Run that way it checks its 98 listed quotes and the 93 quote-and-link pairs in this document, and all pass.

**Caveats:**
- One coder per lens plus one recount coder, with no second rater (every judgement is stored and auditable).
- The HN comments were selected by ownership-related searches, so they over-represent owners and self-hosters. Use them for reasons and wording, never for prevalence.
- **The three lenses agree with one another on the same data.** All three coded this HN-heavy, ownership-searched sample, so their agreement is not independent market evidence.
- App Store and Google Play reviewers skew toward free-tier listeners, and almost none of them write about files they own: 0 of the 116 free-tier-control reviewers the fit check read do, and 1 of the 12 store rows in the own-files themes is about owned files.
- **One google-play text appears three times.** Three near-identical reviews on the IN listing (play.google.com … hl=en_IN `#review-1` 2025-08-29, `#review-14` 2026-07-09, `#review-2` 2026-09-15; word overlap 0.85–0.87) are counted separately in free-tier control and ads, and in the switching-intent list. Deduplicated, google-play falls by 2 in each (free-tier control 26 → 24).
- Some HN complaints predate fixes in the original (lossless before 2025-09, folders before 2026-05), and some HN quotes are about other services; both are marked where they are quoted.
- The keyword ranker in `replica/feedback.md` counts loose regex hits across all ratings (for example "Local files and my own music" 64, against 34 here). Treat it as a starting point only.

## 5. Fix plan

Eight fixes, each checked against `analysis/facts/spotify-changelog.verified.json` and the 4 support pages the fit check fetched on 2026-10-04, so that nothing the original already ships is sold as a fix. After review each fix carries a type: **policy** (an internal rule, never a headline), **hygiene** (the original has it; the difference is a default), **table stakes** (lockers or self-hosting already offer it), or **difference** (not found in the original or in the competitors checked). features.csv is read-only in this phase; proposed rows and notes are in `analysis/features-rows.final.json`.

**Hard gate (section 6):** the landing-page and waitlist test runs first, because it needs no product. The gate covers L-sized storage, transcode and offline work only: F5, the F6 bulk and transcode pipeline and F7's full-file export start only after it passes. The thin vertical slice that /replica-architect defines (single-file upload, library, player) is built first. F1–F4, F8 and F7's playlist and history export are not gated; they come after the slice.

| fix | type | size | skills | waits for the gate? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 every control on every plan | policy | S | /replica-launch, /replica-backend | no |
| F2 playlists play only their own tracks | hygiene (default off) | S | /replica-build | no |
| F3 a quiet, music-only app | table stakes (retention) | S | /replica-design, /replica-build, /replica-brand | no |
| F4 shuffle for large collections | difference against the original (a stated once-per-cycle rule on every plan); unverified against lockers | M | /replica-architect, then /replica-build, /replica-backend | no |
| F5 offline downloads of own files | table stakes for owners | L | /replica-build, /replica-backend, /replica-architect, /replica-launch | yes |
| F6 the locker: bulk upload, stored copy kept | must, table stakes | L | /replica-backend, /replica-build, /replica-architect | yes (beyond the single-file slice) |
| F7 always exportable, time-boxed over quota | partly shipped by the original; difference = audio files and standard formats | M–L | /replica-backend, /replica-launch, counsel | file export at scale: yes |
| F8 collector tools | partly shipped; difference = personal in-app play counts and live smart playlists | M | /replica-build, /replica-backend | no |

**F1. Every control on every plan (internal entitlement policy, not a headline).** Size **S**. Skill: **/replica-launch** (plan matrix and price-change policy), **/replica-backend** (entitlement list).
- Build or change: no plan limits picking a track, skips, play in order, repeat, seek, queue or drag-reorder of the user's own files, and the player does not check the plan for these. Plans differ by storage, streaming quality, seats and the number of devices that hold downloads (F5 explains why downloads themselves are not the gate; /replica-launch can change that only under F5's switch rule). Publish a price-change policy (advance notice; existing subscribers keep their price for a set period).
- Not a headline or proof point. Aimed at the mass market, "unlimited skips on every plan" reads as free on-demand music, which is an implied catalogue promise. Don't echo the original's Premium wording, which sells «Repeat songs, play albums in any order, and skip as many times as you want.» (spotify.com/us/premium, fetched 2026-10-04). Against the original this differs only from its free tier (its mobile queue is labelled Premium, support 2025-06-30, and mobile drag-reorder is Premium only, support 2026-02-26). Lockers don't gate these controls, so against them it is table stakes.
- Evidence and whose problem: free-tier control 117 (2 sources: app-store 91, google-play 26); queue 16 (3 sources); price increases 31 (3 sources). These are free catalogue listeners, and 0 of the 116 the fit check read own files. The rule costs tunebox no royalties, but the quote shows their problem, not an owner's: “you have to pay for your playlist to be lay in order” ([app-store, 5★, 2026-10-01](https://itunes.apple.com/gb/rss/customerreviews/page=2/id=324684580/sortby=mostrecent/json#review-14613907575)) (a free-tier listener).

## 6. Angle

**A. Own it, play it everywhere (the recommended category).**
For people who own their music (CD rips, downloads they bought, tracks that were never on streaming) and don't want to run a server, tunebox stores their own files in the cloud with the stored copy kept bit for bit, plays them on web, iPhone and Android, including downloads for offline listening, in the order they chose, and keeps the whole library exportable, with N days' notice before any deletion for plan or account reasons (copyright takedowns follow the DMCA process). It sits alongside whatever they stream; it doesn't replace it (U6).

*Evidence, stated honestly:* the own-files group is 67 core reviews (upload, own rather than rent, self-hosting effort), and 59 of them are HN comments from a sample searched for ownership. Of the 12 store rows in the own-files themes, 1 is about owned files. Across all 1088 store reviews, none clearly asks to upload files the reviewer legally holds (owner-keyword net, 13 hits, all read). Upload demand itself is 31 reviews, 28 of them HN, with store support essentially absent. **So the demand is thin outside HN, and its size is unknown.** Owners compare tunebox with self-hosting and with the catalogue apps that accept uploads (Apple Music 15, Plex 12, YouTube Music 11, Navidrome 8 mentions), not with small lockers. A gets tunebox into the category; on its own it does not differentiate it, because YouTube Music uploads (free), iBroadcast (free), VOX ($4.99/month) and Apple's cloud library already sell the core promise.

*Target first:* would-be self-hosters who don't want the server work (U1), and, as a thin HN signal, former Google Play Music uploaders unhappy with YouTube Music's uploads.

**HARD GATE: no L-sized storage, transcode or offline work** (F5, the F6 bulk and transcode pipeline, F7's full-file export) **starts until a landing page and waitlist test passes** a threshold that /replica-launch sets and records before the test runs. If it fails, stop or re-position; the evidence here cannot size the market.

*Proof points, measured against lockers and self-hosting (not against the original's free tier):*
1. **Zero setup, no server.** Plexamp needs a user-run Plex Media Server, Navidrome has no upload by design, and Subsonic clients such as Amperfy need a private server (official listings and docs). “if something takes more than a few clicks to go from zero to music that leaves 95% of users out!” ([hacker-news, no rating, 2026-09-01](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49518972)) (also in U1). Caveat: Navidrome's docs point to managed hosting (PikaPods, Zenith), which narrows this gap, though uploads still need separate tools.
2. **Whole-folder upload, originals kept** (F6). Parity with VOX on keeping FLAC; a difference against Navidrome (no upload) and the original (no cloud).
3. **Always exportable, with notice** (F7): playlists, history and audio files in standard formats; one stated rule after a downgrade or cancel (files within the new plan's quota (the free quota after a cancel) stay; the excess keeps playback, downloads and export for N days before deletion after notice); and N days' notice before any deletion for plan or account reasons, or a shutdown (copyright takedowns follow the DMCA process, section 7). Against the history of Google Play Music (deleted 2021) and Amazon (closed 2017).
4. **Shuffle and history built for large collections** (F4, F8): once-per-cycle shuffle across devices, personal in-app play counts, live smart playlists. Navidrome has per-user play counts; the bet is having them without a server.

Each proof point is matched by someone on its own. The bet is the combination without a server, and that bet is what the waitlist test checks. F1–F3 are hygiene and table stakes: they keep owners, they don't win them.

*Risks:* thin, HN-only demand (above). Free incumbents cap the price: YouTube Music uploads (free to play, ad-free and offline, per a user quoting Google's help page), iBroadcast (free, no ads); VOX is the paid anchor at $4.99/month, unlimited. Storage and egress costs grow with FLAC collections. User uploads bring copyright exposure (section 7). There is no discovery (U6). Lockers have shut down before, so trust depends on F7.

**B. "Your music, your rules": voice only, not an angle.**
Evidence: free-tier control 117 (2 sources: app-store 91, google-play 26; none from HN), plus songs injected 30 and shuffle 25. 0 of the 116 free-tier-control reviewers the fit check read own files. Aimed at mass-market listeners, B reads as free on-demand music, which is an implied catalogue promise, and nothing in either verified market file shows a locker that mixes in songs, caps skips or gates controls, so for owners these points are parity. The original also keeps closing the gap (free Search & Play 2025-09, Fewer Repeats 2025-11, Smart Shuffle off for Premium, reshuffle 2026-05). Use the tone ("in your order", "only what you added") in product copy and onboarding. Never use it as a headline or a proof point, and never write "unlimited skips on every plan" in mass-market copy. Comparative copy that names the original carries trademark and store copycat risk.

**C. Offline: a feature line under A, not an angle.**
26 of the 27 offline complaints are about the original's catalogue downloads; only 1 is about Local Files. For owners, the original's Local Files already play offline, YouTube Music uploads play offline without Premium, and Plexamp has downloads. It is also L-sized native work. Under A, write it as a plain feature line ("download your own files to your phone for offline listening"), with no reliability superlatives. F5 holds the one offline model.

**Recommendation: lead with A as the category, and test it before building the heavy parts.**
- A is the only angle where buyer and product match, and where tunebox's core feature (upload) is the answer rather than the absence of something the original does.
- Use the four locker-relative proof points above. B is voice only; C is a feature line under A.
- The three lenses agree on A, but they coded the same HN-heavy, ownership-searched sample, so their agreement is not independent evidence. Apply the thin rule consistently: A's upload demand is thin outside HN, and so is the Subsonic-compatible API, which stays on the table as a /replica-architect option (section 5).
- The landing page and waitlist test is a hard gate, not a suggestion (above).
- No original's name in tunebox's name, ads or store listing. Avoid other trademarks in ads too (for example "the Google Play Music replacement").
