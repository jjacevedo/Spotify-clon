# tunebox: what users of the original hate, miss and leave unsolved, and what tunebox does about it

Final, 2026-10-04. /replica-entrepreneur research document. Three analyst lenses (evidence, fit, market) coded the same data, then three adversarial verifiers (quotes, counts, fit) checked the draft. Every verifier finding is applied, and so are the findings of two independent audits of the final document (7 each) and the 14 findings of a final business and legal review; section 8 lists every round. tunebox has no licensed catalogue. It only plays files the user uploaded (web, iOS, Android).

Reviewers' words below are research, never testimonials: none of them may appear on a landing page, store listing or ad. Every review quote (curly quotes followed by a link) is an exact substring of the `text` cell of `replica/reviews.csv` for the linked row. Text copied from official pages is shown between « and » with its URL and date. This document names the original because it is research; tunebox's name, ads and store listing must not.

**Method:**
- Three analysts each read the reviews by hand and used keyword nets only to find candidates. The **evidence** lens read all 594 "core" reviews (store reviews rated 1–3★: app-store 409, google-play 46; plus all 139 HN comments). It also scanned all 633 reviews rated 4–5★ (app-store 619, google-play 14) for *love it, but…* complaints in 25 themes. It checked the precision and recall of every keyword set against its hand labels. The **fit** and **market** lenses hand-coded all 1227 rows with their own codebooks.
- **Numbers below are the evidence lens's**, except where the independent recount (next bullet) found rows outside a theme's own definition. Those figures are the recount's and are marked *recount*. Counts are written as *n = core + 4–5★*. "4–5★ not scanned" means that theme was only counted in core. Themes are multi-label, so counts don't add up to the sample. Counts are reviews, not people.
- **Review pass (three adversarial checks).** *Quotes:* re-read the full row behind all 88 review quotes in the draft; all were exact, 3 failed on context and were replaced, 3 got an attribution note. *Counts:* one verifier read all 1227 rows in full with its own codebook (`analysis/verify/labels.txt`), re-read every row where its labels and the evidence lens's lists disagreed, and recorded each accept or reject with a reason (`analysis/verify/vcorrect.py`, output `analysis/verify-counts.json`). *Fit:* hand-read the own-files store rows, the injection, offline and shuffle themes and every owner-signal keyword hit, and fetched 4 more official support pages on 2026-10-04 (Autoplay, Understanding your data, Data rights and privacy choices, Recent activity; `analysis/verify-fit-raw/`). I spot-checked the recount's disputed rows for injection, library tools, clutter, icon, lossless, car, upload and discovery against `reviews.csv` and found no reason to depart from its figures.
- **Synthesizer re-run** (`analysis/synth/`): `compare.py` joins the three lenses theme by theme on review URL. The fit lens uses 1-based row ids and the other two use 0-based ids, so URL is the only safe join key. The 160 unique rows that fit or market placed in a theme where the evidence lens did not were re-read across the 8 most disputed themes (`adjudicate.py`); 4 were added (2 to playlist injection, 2 to free-tier control). `recount.py` re-derives the evidence-lens counts, source splits and date splits (`recount.json`). `extra_themes.py` counts two small themes used only in section 4. `quotes.py` verifies every quote.
- **Thin rule:** fewer than 3 reviews, or only one source, is marked **thin**. A theme whose store rows, on reading, do not actually express the theme is treated as single-source; this applies to upload demand (section 3, item 1). Thin themes are never presented as trends. The rule is applied the same way to tunebox's own bet (Angle A). It applies to demand claims only: the Subsonic-compatible API is a way of delivering the product, so it is judged on build cost and time to first revenue instead (section 5).
- **Can tunebox address it?** "Yes" means tunebox owns that part of its own product (player, library, UX, pricing). "No, position only" means the complaint comes from the licensed catalogue, the ads business, artist payouts or politics. tunebox can only position against those, carefully.

## 5. Fix plan

Eight fixes, each checked against `analysis/facts/spotify-changelog.verified.json` and the 4 support pages the fit check fetched on 2026-10-04, so that nothing the original already ships is sold as a fix. After review each fix carries a type: **policy** (an internal rule, never a headline), **hygiene** (the original has it; the difference is a default), **table stakes** (lockers or self-hosting already offer it), or **difference** (not found in the original or in the competitors checked). The rows and notes in `analysis/features-rows.final.json` were applied to features.csv in commit efebd5a (82 rows); the final review's note changes are in both files (section 8, D-lines).

**Hard gate (section 6):** the landing-page test runs first, because it needs no product, and nothing beyond the landing page is built until its result is recorded. The thin vertical slice that /replica-architect defines (single-file upload, library, player) is built first after the gate result is recorded, and only on a pass; F1–F4, F8 and F7's playlist and history export follow it; the L-sized storage, transcode and offline work (F5, the F6 bulk and transcode pipeline, F7's full-file export) comes last. An ambiguous result allows one re-run with a changed price or copy. A fail means stop, or a re-test of the cheaper Subsonic-compatible backend (flagged below) before any native build.

| fix | type | size | skills | order after a pass |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F1 every control on every plan | policy | S | /replica-launch, /replica-backend | after the slice |
| F2 playlists play only their own tracks | hygiene (default off) | S | /replica-build | after the slice |
| F3 a quiet, music-only app | table stakes (retention) | S | /replica-design, /replica-build, /replica-brand | after the slice |
| F4 shuffle for large collections | difference against the original (a stated once-per-cycle rule on every plan); unverified against lockers | M | /replica-architect, then /replica-build, /replica-backend | after the slice |
| F5 offline downloads of own files | table stakes for owners | L | /replica-build, /replica-backend, /replica-architect, /replica-launch | last (L-sized) |
| F6 the locker: bulk upload, stored copy kept | must, table stakes | L | /replica-backend, /replica-build, /replica-architect | single-file upload in the slice; bulk and transcode pipeline last |
| F7 exportable on demand, time-boxed over quota | partly shipped by the original; difference = audio files and standard formats | M–L | /replica-backend, /replica-launch, counsel | playlist and history export after the slice; full-file export last |
| F8 collector tools | partly shipped; difference = personal in-app play counts and live smart playlists | M | /replica-build, /replica-backend | after the slice |

**F1. Every control on every plan (internal entitlement policy, not a headline).** Size **S**. Skill: **/replica-launch** (plan matrix and price-change policy), **/replica-backend** (entitlement list).

## 6. Angle

**A. Own it, play it everywhere (the recommended category).**
For people who own their music (CD rips, downloads they bought, tracks that were never on streaming) and don't want to run a server, tunebox stores their own files in the cloud with the stored copy kept bit for bit, plays them on web, iPhone and Android, including downloads for offline listening, in the order they chose, and keeps the whole library exportable on demand, with N days' notice before a deletion for plan reasons, a user's own account deletion or a shutdown (copyright takedowns follow the notice-and-takedown process, section 7). It sits alongside whatever they stream; it doesn't replace it (U6).

*Evidence, stated honestly:* the own-files group is 67 core reviews (upload, own rather than rent, self-hosting effort), and 59 of them are HN comments from a sample searched for ownership. Of the 12 store rows in the own-files themes, 1 is about owned files. Across all 1088 store reviews, none clearly asks to upload files the reviewer legally holds (owner-keyword net, 13 hits, all read). Upload demand itself is 31 reviews, 28 of them HN, with store support essentially absent. **So the demand is thin outside HN, and its size is unknown.** Owners compare tunebox with self-hosting and with the catalogue apps that accept uploads (Apple Music 15, Plex 12, YouTube Music 11, Navidrome 8 mentions), not with small lockers. A gets tunebox into the category; on its own it does not differentiate it, because YouTube Music uploads (free), iBroadcast (free), VOX ($4.99/month) and Apple's cloud library already sell the core promise.

*Target first:* would-be self-hosters who don't want the server work (U1), and, as a thin HN signal, former Google Play Music uploaders unhappy with YouTube Music's uploads.

**HARD GATE: nothing beyond the landing page is built until a landing-page test result is recorded, and no L-sized storage, transcode or offline work** (F5, the F6 bulk and transcode pipeline, F7's full-file export) **starts unless it passes.** The evidence here cannot size the market, so the test has to. /replica-launch drafts the gate spec, and the founder approves and records it before the test runs:
1. The page shows the price and storage tiers set from the cost model (section 7) and only the proof points that survive the locker check (below). It says plainly that the product is not built yet.
2. The primary metric is the share of unique visitors who make a paid commitment (a refundable deposit, or "reserve at $X/month" with a card). Email signups are secondary: a free signup cannot tell tunebox apart from free iBroadcast or YouTube Music uploads.
3. Traffic comes from at least two channels other than Hacker News, with a minimum number of unique visitors per channel and a minimum run (for example 4 weeks), both set in advance. Hacker News traffic would repeat this sample's ownership-searched bias.
4. The form asks library size (tracks or GB), main format (MP3/AAC/FLAC) and what the person uses now. The answers replace the market lens's guesses in the cost model.
5. Pass, ambiguous and fail are written down in advance as numbers, and the pass threshold is set to beat the case against building (Risks, below). Pass: build the slice, then F1–F4 and F8, then the gated items. Ambiguous: one re-run with a changed price or copy. Fail: stop, or re-test the cheaper Subsonic-compatible backend (section 5) before any native build. "Stop" is an acceptable outcome.

*Proof points, measured against lockers and self-hosting (not against the original's free tier); each goes on the landing page only if it survives the locker check below:*
1. **Zero setup, no server.** Plexamp needs a user-run Plex Media Server, Navidrome has no upload by design, and Subsonic clients such as Amperfy need a private server (official listings and docs). “if something takes more than a few clicks to go from zero to music that leaves 95% of users out!” ([hacker-news, no rating, 2026-09-01](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=49518972)) (also in U1). Caveat: Navidrome's docs point to managed hosting (PikaPods, Zenith), which narrows this gap, though uploads still need separate tools. This point separates tunebox only from self-hosting: iBroadcast, VOX and YouTube Music uploads need no server either.
2. **Whole-folder upload, originals kept** (F6). Parity with VOX on keeping FLAC; a difference against Navidrome (no upload) and the original (no cloud).
3. **Exportable on demand, with notice** (F7): playlists, history and audio files in standard formats; one stated rule after a downgrade or cancel (files within the new plan's quota (the free quota after a cancel) stay; the excess keeps playback, downloads and export for N days before deletion after notice); and N days' notice before a deletion for plan reasons, a user's own account deletion or a shutdown (copyright takedowns follow the notice-and-takedown process, section 7). Against the history of Google Play Music (deleted 2021) and Amazon (closed 2017).
4. **Shuffle and history built for large collections** (F4, F8): once-per-cycle shuffle across devices, personal in-app play counts, live smart playlists. Navidrome has per-user play counts; the bet is having them without a server.

*Locker check, before the landing page is written.* Each proof point is matched by someone on its own, and "without a server" separates tunebox only from self-hosting: iBroadcast (free), VOX ($4.99) and YouTube Music uploads (free) need no server either. *Risks:* thin, HN-only demand (above).
